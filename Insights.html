Industry News

Global hotel chains step up India presence as luxury demand rises

Multiple global hotel chains are gearing up to establish their presence in India, while current hospitality brands are rolling out new luxury and lifestyle properties. Dusit made its much-anticipated return to India last year, and Hyatt is actively exploring potential sites. Both Marriott and Hilton are set to introduce fresh hotel brands in the market, alongside IHG Hotels & Resorts and ITC Hotels, who are also launching exciting new offerings.

Technology & Hospitality

Hotel AI is only as good as the data it's built upon

Hotels have invested heavily in AI, yet many projects will stall before they deliver. The reason is rarely the models. It's the underlying data, consisting of siloed systems, patchy integrations and no single source of truth. Intelligence built on fragmented data produces fragmented decisions.

What stops hotels from building the data foundation AI actually needs, and who in the organization should own it?

Guest Experience

How Guest Expectations Are Changing in India

India is an interesting market because people are discovering and choosing hospitality brands digitally more than ever, while the industry itself is still very relationship-driven. That makes the connection between the two important. A guest might find a restaurant online, but when they actually visit, the welcome, hospitality, atmosphere and overall experience still need to live up to what they saw before coming in.

For me, the opportunity isn't to make hospitality more digital just because we can. It's to make the digital and physical parts of the experience feel like they belong to the same brand.

What This Means for Hospitality

When I look at where hospitality is headed, I think the bigger shift is in what we now consider part of the guest experience. It is no longer just the time spent at a restaurant or hotel. Every interaction with a brand adds to what a guest expects from it.

That also means the bar is getting higher. Guests are becoming more accustomed to experiences that are convenient, relevant and connected, and those expectations will naturally carry into hospitality.

For me, the opportunity is to look at all these touchpoints as one experience, rather than treating them as separate parts of the guest journey. The brands that can bring these pieces together in a way that feels seamless to the guest will be better placed to meet how people discover, choose and experience hospitality today.

Travel Trends

World Tourism Day 2026: The Indian traveller no longer wants a hotel to tell them how to holiday

Indian travellers are moving away from rigid, pre-scripted itineraries. They want hotels to respect their time, routines and choices, making flexibility, convenience and personalisation increasingly important today.

That shift is reshaping the hospitality industry. Guests are not looking for a property that dictates the pace of their trip; they are looking for a place that supports the way they already travel, work and unwind.

Hospitality Strategy

Why flexibility is becoming the new luxury in Indian hospitality

Indian travellers want hotels to respect their time, routines and choices, making flexibility, convenience and personalisation increasingly important today. The most successful hospitality brands are responding by designing experiences that feel intuitive rather than imposed.

This means fewer rigid schedules, more self-directed guest journeys and service that adapts to each traveller's rhythm. From check-in to dining, wellness and room experience, the hospitality proposition is becoming more personalised and less prescriptive.

In an era where guests expect seamless, relevant experiences, the brands that succeed will be the ones that empower rather than instruct.
